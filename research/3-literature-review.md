3 Literature review

3.1 Selection Principle

The review includes eight scientific publications and related official sources on systems and technologies. The scientific core pertains to 2023–2026; the work of Sailer and Homner (2020) is retained as a baseline meta-analysis. Selection is thematic: this is not a claimed exhaustive systematic review. Searches covered gamification in higher education, digital badges, micro-credentials, and extracurricular activities. Publisher pages and available texts were used. For each source, the approach, findings, transfer limitations to AIQAS, and corresponding design decision are highlighted.

Reviews, meta-analyses, and studies allowing justification of AIQAS functions or constraints were included. Promotional forecasts and materials without a clear source were excluded. Technical documentation is considered separately and does not replace scientific publications. Findings from academic learning research are transferred to extracurricular activities only as hypotheses for testing.



Source and Method | Main Finding | Limitation for AIQAS | Project Decision

[L1] Khaldi et al., 2023. Review of 90 studies | Examines game elements and design approaches for gamification. | E-learning context; presence of points does not prove increased event participation. | Justify each mechanics by purpose and evaluate repeat usage.

[L2] Almeida et al., 2023. Mapping 87 papers and focus group | Describes unwanted effects of points, badges, leaderboards, and competitions. | Mapping does not provide a unified risk size for our campus. | Do not introduce a mandatory public ranking; check for duplicates and cheating.

[L3] Ahsan et al., 2023. Review of 56 publications | Systematizes implementation of micro-credentials and digital badges in universities. | Micro-credential is tied to an assessed learning outcome; a ticket itself is not such an outcome. | Separate participation, role, and verified skill.

[L4] Varadarajan et al., 2023. Review of stakeholder interests | Describes expectations of students, universities, employers, and government, trust issues. | Conclusions on micro-credentials do not confirm the value of any attendance badge. | For achievements, record author, criteria, and issuance rationale.

[L5] Zeng et al., 2024. Meta-analysis of 22 experiments | Identifies a positive cumulative effect of gamification on academic performance. | Academic performance differs from attendance; effect size cannot be transferred to AIQAS. | Evaluate own participation metrics, do not promise academic performance growth.

[L6] Rohozneanu et al., 2025. Review of 21 articles | Summarizes positive associations of extracurricular participation with student development metrics. | Correlations and varied contexts do not prove a causal effect of our platform. | Record verified participation; do not evaluate competencies by attendance count.

[L7] Gyedu et al., 2026. Meta-analysis of 22 studies | Reveals positive cumulative effect on motivation and deep learning. | High study heterogeneity and limited longitudinal observations; results are not universal. | Do not promise effect without a pilot; separately measure repeat usage and satisfaction.

[L8] Sailer & Homner, 2020. Baseline meta-analysis | Separates cognitive, motivational, and behavioral outcomes of gamification. | Earlier work; effects depend on design and context. | Separately measure behavior and satisfaction; do not count XP as proof of learning.



3.2 Synthesis and Research Question

Literature allows considering gamification and digital achievements as potential means to support participation, but not as a guaranteed effect [L1, L2, L5, L7, L8]. For AIQAS, a transparent core scenario and verifiable data are primary; reward mechanics should be evaluated separately from eliminating manual operations.

Publications on micro-credentials emphasize the importance of criteria and trust [L3, L4]. Therefore, the record "attended event" must differ from "confirmed skill according to criteria". Open Badges [T10] is considered as possible future development, without claiming already implemented compatibility.

Thesis research question: to what extent does a single digital event cycle reduce organizer workload and accounting errors compared to the current process, while maintaining access control and registration correctness? Additional question for P1: is verified participation history useful for students and does it lead to repeat usage?
